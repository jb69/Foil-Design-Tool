Exact dialog labels, limits and defaults (min / default / max). Use these labels when recommending a change. The meaning column is the Onshape tooltip. Some fields only appear when a checkbox or dropdown above them enables them.

## Universal B-Series Blade Engine

### General

| Label | Min / default / max | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Diameter (D) | 50 / 140 / 1000 mm | Tip-to-tip diameter of the finished propeller. The blade runs from 0.1 x D to 0.5 x D from the shaft axis.Typical efoil: 140-200 mm. Accepts 50-1000 mm. |
| Hub diameter | 10 / 52 / 600 mm | Diameter of the hub this blade joins. Sets where root thickness is measured and where the taper and skew start from. The blade always runs in to r/R = 0.2 so the union with the hub leaves no gap.Typical: 0.3-0.4 x D (52 mm on a 150 mm prop). Accepts 10-600 mm, and must be 0.22-0.6 x D. |
| P/D ratio | 0.2 / 0.743 / 2.0 | Pitch divided by diameter, quoted at 0.7R as in the B-series charts. Sets the twist: each section sits at atan(pitch / 2 x pi x r), steep at the root, flat at the tip.Typical efoil: 0.7-0.9. Lower for more thrust at low speed, higher for top speed. Accepts 0.2-2.0. |
| EAR | 0.1 / 0.552 / 1.3 | Expanded area ratio: blade area of the whole propeller over disk area. Higher gives wider blades - more grip and less cavitation, more drag.Typical efoil: 0.45-0.65. Ceiling is set by the hub: the blades touch when Z x chord exceeds the hub circumference, which the feature warns about. Accepts 0.1-1.3. |
| Blades (chord) | 2 / 3 / 7 | Number of blades in the finished propeller. Used ONLY to divide EAR into one blade's chord and to pick the 2-3 or 4-7 blade outline table - this feature always builds one blade, which you pattern yourself.Typical efoil: 3. At 2 the chord doubles and a wide blade may fail to loft - lower EAR to suit. Accepts 2-7. |
| Direction | dropdown: CW from astern (right-hand), CCW from astern (left-hand) (default CW from astern (right-hand)) | Which way the propeller turns, seen from astern (behind the propeller, looking forward along the direction of travel, +Z). CW from astern is a right-handed propeller, the marine convention for a single screw; CCW builds its true mirror image. Thrust is toward the direction of travel either way; the finished part is shown aft up (tail toward +Z), as it sits on a bench. Match it to the motor direction set in the ESC (an outrunner runs either way; swap two phases to reverse it). A twin or counter-rotating pair uses one of each.Typical: CW (right-hand). Accepts CW or CCW. |

### Operating check

| Label | Min / default / max | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Motor kV | 0 / 0 / 1000 | Motor kV (rpm per volt, no load), the start of the operating check: no-load speed is kV x pack voltage / gear ratio, and the torque constant is taken as 9.55 / kV Nm per amp. Leave at 0 and no check is run. With it and the fields below, the check prints the static and cruise operating points to the notices panel.Typical efoil: 100-160 kV on 12-14S (140 kV on the 63100 and 6384). Accepts 0-1000. |
| Cells (series) | 1 / 12 / 30 | Battery cells in series. Pack voltage is cells x Cell V, so 12S at 3.7 V = 44.4 V.Typical efoil: 12-14S. Accepts 1-30. |
| Cell V | 3.0 / 3.7 / 4.2 | Volts per cell to take the pack at, under load. Sets the no-load speed (kV x cells x this) and the back-EMF headroom, so it moves the top speed and the cruise current.Typical: 3.7 for a mid-discharge nominal figure; 4.0-4.1 for a fresh pack under load (what a VESC log shows on the first run); 3.4 near empty. Accepts 3.0-4.2 V. |
| Torque const | 5 / 9.55 / 12 | Motor torque constant x kV, in Nm per amp times rpm per volt: the torque per amp is this divided by the kV. 9.55 is the ideal 60 / (2 pi) that a datasheet's mNm/A x kV comes to (the Flipsky 6384: 67.4 mNm/A x 140 kV = 9.44); 8.27 was the older figure this check used, which read 13 % low against measured static thrust.Typical: 9.55, or the datasheet's mNm/A x kV / 1000. Accepts 5-12. |
| ESC limit, A | 1 / 100 / 600 | Motor (phase) current limit set in the ESC. Torque at the limit is the torque constant x this current, and the check reports the rpm at which the propeller absorbs exactly that torque, static and at cruise.Typical: 80-150 A (100 A on a Mini FSESC 6.8, 150 A on the Plus). Accepts 1-600 A. |
| Gear ratio | 1 / 1 / 10 | Motor turns per propeller turn. Divides the no-load speed and multiplies the shaft torque.Typical: 1 for a direct-drive outrunner pod; 4 to 5 on a geared inrunner drive. Accepts 1-10. |
| Phase R, mOhm | 0 / 0 / 500 | Motor phase resistance in milliohms. With it, the cruise point accounts for back-EMF: as rpm rises the voltage left to push current falls, so the motor cannot hold the current limit near no-load speed.Typical: 20-50 mOhm on a 63-series outrunner (31 on the 63100). Accepts 0-500; 0 assumes the current limit is available at any rpm, which flatters the cruise figure. |
| Cruise, km/h | 0 / 30 / 100 | Boat speed through the water to report the cruise operating point at, alongside the static case. Thrust and efficiency depend strongly on it through the advance ratio J = V / (n x D).Typical: 20-30 km/h for an efoil, 25-40 km/h for a fast foil. Accepts 0-100 km/h. |
| Cruise drag, N | 0 / 0 / 2000 | The foil's total drag at the cruise speed, in newtons, from the foil tool's performance sweep (drag_total_N at that speed) or a log. With it the check adds the steady cruise point, where thrust equals drag: prop rpm, efficiency, motor amps and volts, throttle, motor input power and Wh/km, and the cavitation number there rather than at full throttle. 0 skips it.Typical: 80-120 N for an efoil at 20-25 km/h. Accepts 0-2000 N. |
| Depth | 100 / 600 / 3000 mm | Depth of the shaft axis below the surface when riding, for the cavitation number: (atmospheric + head - vapour pressure) over the dynamic pressure at 0.7R. Deeper helps a little; the dynamic pressure dominates.Typical: 500-700 mm on an efoil mast. Accepts 100-3000 mm. |

### Advanced

| Label | Min / default / max | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Tip pitch, % | -30 / -10 / 30 | Pitch at the tip relative to nominal. Held at nominal out to 0.7R, then blended parabolically to the tip.Typical: -10 %. Go to -15 or -20 % if the prop ventilates near the surface or sounds harsh; 0 gives constant pitch. Positive loads the tip and is rarely wanted. Accepts -30 to 30 %. |
| Skew (+ back) | -60 / 0 / 60 deg | Sweeps the blade around the shaft, from zero at the hub surface to the full angle at the tip. Positive sweeps back against rotation, which softens the load as each blade passes the mast.Typical: 0-25 deg; use more only to set how folding blades rest. Accepts -60 to 60 deg. |
| Root thick. | 1 / 7 / 50 mm | Maximum section thickness where the blade leaves the hub - the main strength number. Inside the hub it keeps thickening along the same curve.Typical: 6-9 mm on a 150 mm prop, roughly 0.045 x D. Accepts 1-50 mm. |
| Rake (+ aft) | -30 / 15 / 45 deg | Tilts the blade along the shaft axis. Positive leans the tip aft, away from the direction of travel, which relieves root bending stress.Typical: 5-15 deg. Accepts -30 to 45 deg. |
| Taper exponent | 1 / 2 / 3 | How thickness falls from root to tip: t = tip + (root - tip) x (1 - n)^exponent.1 = linear, the B-series form, ~60 % more material at 0.7R. 2 = parabolic, thinner and lower drag. Typical: 1 for a printed blade; thin it toward 2 once one survives a session. Accepts 1-3. |
| Section profile | dropdown: B-series (default), NACA 16, NACA 66-style, Ogival (flat face), Biconvex, NACA 4-digit, Clark Y, Elliptical, Eppler-Shen YS-920, Eppler E817 (default B-series (default)) | Shape of the blade section, the same at every radius. Typical: B-series, the family the chord and pitch tables belong to. B-series uses the family's own ordinates - cambered inboard, flat-faced from about 0.7R out - and ignores the camber input. NACA 16 (crest 0.50c) and NACA 66-style (0.45c) use the a = 0.8 marine mean line. YS-920 and E817 are aft-loaded marine sections, the best bet against cavitation and ventilation. NACA 4-digit and Clark Y (0.30c) are blunter and print-friendly. Ogival, biconvex and elliptical are simple forms. Only the B-series matches the operating check. |
| Camber % | 0 / 2 / 10 | Maximum camber as a percentage of chord, for the NACA and biconvex profiles. Ignored by the B-series, which carries its own camber, and by the ogival profile, whose flat face fixes it.Typical: 2-4 % for a marine section. Higher lifts more at low speed but cavitates sooner. Accepts 0-10 %. |
| Tip thick. | 0.2 / 1.2 / 20 mm | Maximum section thickness at the tip.Typical: 1.2-2 mm, and at least 2-3 extrusion widths. Thin tips are quieter but chip on grounding. Accepts 0.2-20 mm; must be at least the edge thickness. |
| Edge thick. | 0.2 / 1.2 / 10 mm | Height of the square-cut trailing edge, and the thickness the rounded leading-edge nose is built from.Typical: 0.8-1.2 mm with a 0.4 mm nozzle - two to three extrusion widths. Below 0.8 mm the edge will not print as modelled and the root section gets hard to cap. Accepts 0.2-10 mm. |
| Tip chord | 1 / 10 / 200 mm | Blade width at the very tip. The published B-series chord goes to zero there, which cannot be lofted or printed, so this squares it off.Typical: 0.04-0.07 x D (6-10 mm on a 150 mm prop). Larger looks blunt and adds tip vortex. Accepts 1-200 mm, and must be under half the chord at 0.9R. |
| Round tip | checkbox, default on | Closes the blade with an elliptical dome instead of a flat end face: over the last 2 % of radius chord and thickness scale down together on an ellipse, stopping at 45 % rather than a knife edge. Adds three sections out there.Typical: on. Accepts on or off; turn it off only if the tip loft misbehaves. |
| Sections | 6 / 20 / 60 | Number of section profiles lofted from r/R = 0.2 to the tip, spaced with extra density outboard.Typical: 20. Use 10-12 while iterating for fast regeneration, 25-30 for the final shape. Past 30 the gain is invisible and the loft gets harder to solve. Accepts 6-60. |
| Keep sections | checkbox, default off | Leaves the section curves in the Part Studio alongside the solid, instead of deleting them. Useful for inspecting the shape or taking sections into a drawing.Typical: off. Accepts on or off. |
| Print to notices | checkbox, default off | Prints the inputs, a per-station table (radius, chord, thickness, pitch, wrap angle), each loft stage and the operating check to the FeatureScript notices panel and to the Part Studio variables propLog and propNotice. With it on, a failure is reported as a warning with its message instead of an error, so the log survives.Typical: on while designing, off for a finished part. Accepts on or off. |
| Sections only | checkbox, default off | Builds the section curves and stops - no loft, no solid. Use it when the loft fails, to see whether the curves themselves are sane.Typical: off. Accepts on or off. |

### Hub

| Label | Min / default / max | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Hub type | dropdown: Fixed blades, Folding blades (default FIXED) | Fixed blades: the blades are joined to the hub with a root fillet - one solid.Folding blades: each blade pivots aft on a tangential pin in a slot of the hub (the Folding group), so the set is one hub body plus Z blade bodies (and Z pin bodies for a straight pin); the blades are shown deployed or folded. The hub is never cut for the blade beyond the tongue slot and the pin holes: the blade's root and fairing are trimmed to the hub's external profile deployed and wherever they would meet the hub through the fold, so a long hub behind the pin costs root area on the blade.Typical: Fixed for a motor-mounted efoil prop; Folding on a foil that is also sailed, paddled or towed. Accepts one of the two. |
| Upstream dia | 10 / 63 / 300 mm | Hub diameter at the upstream face, the one that meets the water first (the direction of travel; the finished part is shown aft up, so upstream points down, -Z). The hub is a straight taper from this to the downstream diameter.Typical: the motor's own diameter, 63 mm on a 63-series outrunner. Accepts 10-300 mm. |
| Downstream dia | 10 / 50 / 300 mm | Hub diameter at the downstream face.Typical: 0.8 x the upstream diameter, 50 mm on a 63 mm hub; equal for a plain cylinder. Accepts 10-300 mm. |
| Hub length | 5 / 20 / 300 mm | Length of the hub along the shaft, end face to end face, before any nose or tail cap. The blade root plane sits at its middle unless Axial shift moves it.Typical: 20-50 mm. Accepts 5-300 mm. |
| Axial shift | -100 / 0 / 100 mm | Moves the hub along the shaft relative to the blade root plane: positive upstream (toward the direction of travel, shown pointing down). 0 centres the hub on the blades.Typical: 0; a few mm aft when the blades are raked and the tips would overhang the downstream face. Accepts -100 to 100 mm. |
| Nose cap | 0 / 0 / 200 mm | A cone on the upstream face, from the upstream diameter down to a fifth of it over this length. 0 leaves the face flat, which is right when the hub sits against the motor bell.Typical: 0 against a motor; 0.25-0.5 x the hub diameter for a free nose. Accepts 0-200 mm. |
| Tail style | dropdown: Flat face, Cone cap (convex), Dish (concave) (default Flat face) | What the downstream end of the hub does.Flat face: the hub ends square.Cone cap: a convex cone out of the face, Tail cap long, closing the wake behind a hub that hangs in the flow.Dish: a concave cone cut into the face, from the downstream rim in to the tail pocket at Dish angle from the face - the printed hub loses the mass it does not need, the bolt heads sit inside the dish, and the water leaves the rim cleanly. Tail pocket and Pocket floor apply to every style.Typical: Dish behind a motor bell; Cone cap on a hub that ends in the flow. Accepts one of the three. |
| Tail cap | 1 / 25 / 200 mm | Length of the convex cone on the downstream face, from the downstream diameter down to a fifth of it.Typical: 0.5-1 x the downstream diameter. Accepts 1-200 mm. |
| Dish angle | 5 / 30 / 60 deg | Angle of the dish's cone from the downstream face plane: 0 would be flat, 90 a straight bore. The dish rises from the rim toward the axis by (rim radius - pocket radius) x tan(angle); it must stay clear of the upstream face by the pocket floor, or the feature warns and flattens it.Typical: 30 deg (the template). Accepts 5-60 deg. |
| Tail pocket | 0 / 0 / 200 mm | Diameter of a pocket cut into the downstream end along the axis, up to the pocket floor: room for the shaft end, a nut or a circlip, and less printed mass. 0 for none. With a bore or centre bore as well, the bore continues through the floor.Typical: 24 mm over a 10 mm shaft end (the template); 0 on a solid hub. Accepts 0-200 mm. |
| Pocket floor | 2 / 12 / 200 mm | Wall left between the pocket floor and the upstream face - the solid that carries the blades and takes the bolts. Used when Tail pocket is above 0.Typical: 10-14 mm (12 in the template); at least the bolt flange plus a few mm. Accepts 2-200 mm. |
| Root fillet | 0 / 2 / 20 mm | Fillet radius where each blade meets the hub. 0 for none. The fillet is tried last; if the kernel refuses it (grazing angles at high skew or rake), the propeller is built without it and a warning says so.Typical: 2-4 mm. Accepts 0-20 mm. |
| Hub core | checkbox, default off | Also builds the printed core a one-piece carbon propeller is laid up round: the hub inset by Skin thick on its sides and both faces, with the same bore, keyway or bolt circle and the same tail dish and pocket as the hub, as a separate body named Hub core. The Propeller mold reads it for the laminate volume. Off for a printed propeller. |
| Skin thick | 0.5 / 1.5 / 4 mm | Laminate over the core on the hub's sides and faces. Limits 0.5-4 mm. Typical 1.5: seven plies of 200 gsm woven at 50 % fibre volume (0.22 mm each), or three of 450 gsm; the mold's layup line counts them. The dish and the pocket are the core's own bare faces. |

### Folding

| Label | Min / default / max | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Pin type | dropdown: Straight pin, Cap-head bolt and captive nut (default PIN) | What the blade pivots on.Straight pin: a plain pin (dowel, drill rod, stainless pin) pressed into the hub's cheeks and running in the boss; Pin dia, Hub fit and Blade fit size the holes, and a pin body is made per blade for the drawing and the print list.Cap-head bolt and captive nut: a socket head cap screw of Bolt size through the cheeks and the boss, its head in a counterbore on one cheek and a hex nut in a pocket on the other, so the pin can be fitted and removed without a press.Typical: a 4 mm stainless pin, or an M4 bolt. Accepts one of the two. |
| Pin bolt | dropdown: M3, M4, M5, M6, M8, Custom (default M4) | Metric size of the cap-head bolt that is the pin: ISO 273 clearance through the cheeks and the boss, an ISO 4762 head counterbore on one cheek, an ISO 4032 nut pocket (across flats + 0.3 mm, depth + 1) on the other. Custom uses Pin dia as the hole.Typical: M4. Accepts M3-M8 or Custom. |
| Pin dia | 2 / 4 / 12 mm | Diameter of the straight pin, or the hole for a custom bolt.Typical: 4 mm on a 140-160 mm prop, 5 mm on a 200 mm (the geared 52 mm hub uses 5 mm). Accepts 2-12 mm. |
| Hub fit | -0.5 / -0.1 / 0.5 mm | Added to the pin diameter for the holes in the hub's cheeks: negative for a press fit, positive for a clearance so the pin is retained another way.Typical: -0.1 mm for a press fit in a printed cheek (printed holes also come out small; ream if needed). | Blade fit | 0 / 0.2 / 1 mm | Added to the pin diameter for the bore through the blade's boss: the running clearance the blade pivots on.Typical: 0.2 mm printed, 0.1 mm reamed. Accepts 0-1 mm. |
| Pin bodies | checkbox, default on | Makes a pin body per blade, cut flush with the hub surface, so the pins appear in the drawing, the parts list and the assembly.Typical: on. Accepts on or off. |
| Pin radius | 5 / 20 / 250 mm | Distance of the pin axis from the shaft axis. Further out gives a longer hub cheek behind the pin and a shorter tongue; further in leaves more cheek wall.Typical: 0.6-0.7 x the hub radius (20 mm on a 63 mm hub, 17 on a 52). Accepts 5-250 mm. |
| Pin axial | -100 / 0 / 100 mm | Axial offset of the pin from the blade root's generator line, which sits at z = -r x tan(rake) at the root (aft of the root plane on a raked blade): positive upstream. | Boss dia | 5 / 14 / 60 mm | Diameter of the cylindrical boss on the blade root around the pin, and the height of the tongue that joins it to the blade. | Boss width | 3 / 10 / 40 mm | Width of the boss and tongue along the pin: the bearing length on the pin and the thickness that takes the stop load. The hub slot is this plus twice the clearance, so wider bosses leave narrower cheeks.Typical: 8-12 mm on a 63 mm hub. Accepts 3-40 mm. |
| Root fairing | 0 / 8 / 60 mm | Length outside the hub over which the aerofoil is lofted down to the tongue's section (Boss width by Boss dia) at the hub surface, so the root blends into the boss instead of ending in a bare rectangle. Accepts 0-60 mm. |
| Tail relief | checkbox, default on | Turns the hub's trailing end behind the pins down to a round shoulder of the tongue's length about the pin circle, with a core tube behind it (its size set by the pin radius less the root section's reach aft of the pin: Pin axial aft grows it, Tube wall keeps the shaft tube inside it), all the way round, so the blade root - its leading edge above all - swings aft without meeting the cheeks' trailing part; the cheeks keep their pins out to the shoulder. Off leaves the hub whole behind the pins and lets the hub trim the blade root instead.Typical: on. Accepts on or off. |
| Tube wall | 0 / 0 / 10 mm | Wall the hub keeps round its bore through the folding root: a tube of the bore plus this wall each side stays solid through the gap and the tail relief, and the blade - boss, tongue, fairing and root - is trimmed to clear it (plus Clearance) in every position through the fold. Needs a bore (a keyed, plain or piloted mount).Typical: 0; 2-3 mm printed when the core behind the pins is reported thin. Accepts 0-10 mm. |
| Gap width | 4 / 12 / 100 mm | Width, along the pin, of the opening between the hub's cheeks that the blade's boss and root swing through: a flat-walled gap from the tail up to the boss's top plus clearance, whose forward wall is the stop the root bears on under thrust. Never less than the boss plus clearance.Typical: the boss width plus 2-6 mm for the fairing, 12-16 mm on a 63 mm hub; wider on a printed hub with narrow cheeks like the geared 52 mm one. Accepts 4-100 mm. |
| Clearance | 0.1 / 0.3 / 2 mm | Gap between the boss or root and the cheeks, each side, between the root and the hub surface, and between the trimmed root and the hub through the fold.Typical: 0.3 mm printed. Accepts 0.1-2 mm. |
| Fold angle | 30 / 90 / 150 deg | How far the blade swings aft from deployed: the tongue slot is cut to allow it, the blade root is trimmed wherever it would meet the hub on the way, and Show folded displays it. 90 lays the blade along the shaft axis.Typical: 90 deg; 100-110 to tuck the tips in against a tapered hub. Accepts 30-150 deg. |
| Show folded | checkbox, default off | Rotates the blades to Fold angle and checks that they clear the hub and each other; off shows them deployed. Either way the hub and the blades are built the same.Typical: off to design the blade, on to check the fold. Accepts on or off. |
